I study where subjective value is represented in the primate brain, focusing on the interface between visual processing and economic choice.

Research

My dissertation investigates whether subjective value is represented in the inferotemporal cortex (IT), a high-level visual area upstream of the orbitofrontal cortex (OFC). While OFC is well known for encoding the value of choice options, it remains unclear how far upstream value-related signals can be found in the cortical processing hierarchy.

Using single-unit electrophysiology in macaques performing economic choice tasks, I examine whether IT neurons encode subjective value and characterize how these signals relate to the region's representations of visual features, quantity, and spatial information. I also compare value-related activity in IT and OFC to investigate where along this pathway value signals are represented during choice.
